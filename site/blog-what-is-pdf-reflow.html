What is PDF reflow?

The difference between preserving a page and preserving the reason you opened it.

PDF reflow is the process of taking text from a fixed-layout PDF and arranging it into a responsive reading column. Instead of showing the document as a miniature printed page, a reflowed view lets the text adapt to the screen in front of you.

Why PDFs need reflow

A PDF records where things sit on a page: this line begins here, that heading sits above this paragraph, and the footer occupies the bottom margin. That precision is useful when the document needs to print the same way everywhere. It is less useful when you want to read a dense report on a phone.

Without reflow, the reader often has two choices: view the entire page with tiny type or zoom in and drag around the document. Neither choice is a good rhythm for sustained reading.

What a reflowed reader changes

A reflowed PDF reader extracts selectable text and renders it as normal web content. The text can use a comfortable font size, a readable line length, and the full vertical scroll of the screen. Headings can become headings again. Repeated page numbers and running headers can be left behind.

Good reflow is not just a text dump. It needs to reconstruct enough structure that the reader can tell when a chapter, section, or paragraph changes.

What reflow cannot solve

Reflow is not magic. A scanned PDF with no selectable text needs OCR before it can be reflowed. Complex tables, equations, charts, forms, and layouts where visual position carries meaning may be better in the original viewer. A responsible reader should make those boundaries clear.

Reflow changes the surface, not the source. The original document still matters.

When should you use it?

Use reflow when you are reading an essay, book, research paper, report, or other text-heavy document for comprehension. Keep the fixed PDF when you are checking page layout, filling a form, comparing figures, or citing an exact visual location.
