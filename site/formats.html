Paper / supported formats

What Paper reads today — and where it's going.

Paper is deliberately starting with one dependable source: public PDFs that contain selectable text. The reading surface can grow only when each new format deserves the same care.

A clear format boundary is part of a good reading product. Here is what works now, what does not, and which open formats belong to the longer-term direction.

Currently supported

Public PDF URLs

Paste an HTTP or HTTPS link to a PDF that is publicly fetchable. Paper opens the document from the link instead of asking you to create an account or build a library.

Selectable text

The PDF needs a real text layer. Paper can reflow text-heavy books, essays, papers, and reports; scanned image-only PDFs are not OCR'd in the current version.

Up to 30 MB

The current fetcher caps PDFs at 30 MB. This keeps the URL-to-reader path predictable while the product is focused on reading quality.

Reading, not page fidelity

Paper preserves the words and useful hierarchy as a reading view. Keep the original PDF for forms, precise page layout, complex figures, or anything where visual placement is the point.

Future directions

These are areas Paper may explore, not formats supported today and not promises about a release date.

Public-domain books

Standard books from open collections such as Project Gutenberg are a natural extension of the reading-layer idea, especially when a clean book view is available.

EPUB and open ebooks

An open ebook format could fit Paper's typography-first approach, provided import, licensing, and source attribution remain clear.

Long web pages

Essays and documentation already live on the public web. A future reader could make them as calm to read as a reflowed document without copying the web's surrounding noise.

More document types

Other public, text-first formats may follow when Paper can support their structure honestly instead of flattening everything into an unreliable stream.
