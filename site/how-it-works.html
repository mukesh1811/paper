Paper handles the awkward last mile between a long document and a human reader. It keeps the original source in view while giving the text a better place to live.

01 / Bring the link

Paste a public HTTP or HTTPS PDF URL. The Tolstoy sample on the home page is available as a quick fixture if you want to see the flow before finding your own document.

02 / Paper fetches safely

The server fetches the document to avoid browser CORS problems. It rejects credentials, private network targets, unsafe redirects, and PDFs over the 30 MB MVP limit.

03 / Text finds its shape

Paper reads selectable text from the PDF, identifies likely body sizing, detects headings, and filters repeated headers, footers, and page numbers.

04 / The page reflows

Instead of forcing a fixed page onto your screen, Paper renders the extracted text in a responsive column with book-like type and natural scrolling.

05 / You set the rhythm

Use the Aa menu to adjust text size, line width, line spacing, and theme. These preferences stay in your browser for the next document.

06 / Come back later

Paper stores reading progress locally against the URL, so you can close the tab and return to the same place without creating an account.

Why not just use the built-in PDF viewer?

Built-in viewers are good at preserving a page exactly. That is their job. Paper is for the different job: making a long document comfortable to read. When layout fidelity matters most, keep the original PDF. When the words matter most, open it in Paper.
