PDF vs. EPUB: fixed pages and flowing text

The formats feel different because they preserve different relationships between content, page, and screen.

PDF and EPUB are both used for books and long documents, but they begin with different ideas. A PDF is primarily a fixed visual document. An EPUB is a packaged, reflowable publication whose text is expected to adapt to the reading surface.

PDF preserves a page

A PDF tries to keep the same arrangement across devices and printers. Text, images, margins, columns, and page numbers have positions. That makes the format useful when layout is part of the meaning: a signed form, a designed report, a paper with exact citations, or a book prepared for print.

The tradeoff is that a fixed page can become awkward on a small screen. The viewer has to scale the page or let the reader pan around it. A PDF can be beautifully designed and still be a tiring thing to read on a phone.

EPUB lets the text flow

An EPUB is closer to a small website packaged as a book. Text can wrap to the available width, and the reader can usually change type size, margins, and spacing without changing the publication's underlying content. That makes it naturally suited to novels and other text-first books.

Flowing text does not mean an EPUB has no design. It can contain chapters, images, typography, metadata, and navigation. It simply treats the screen as variable rather than treating one printed page as the permanent unit.

Why a PDF can still benefit from reflow

Many public books and papers are available only as PDFs. A reader should not have to reject a useful document because its source format was designed around paper. Reflow can create an EPUB-like reading surface from the PDF's selectable text, even though the source remains a PDF.

That transformation is useful but imperfect. It may lose visual relationships, misread columns, or omit image-only material. It is best understood as a comprehension layer, not as a perfect format conversion.

EPUB starts with flowing text. PDF reflow has to recover a flowing path from a fixed page.

Which one should you choose?

Choose EPUB when you have a legitimate, well-structured ebook and want a flexible book reader. Choose PDF when you need the source's exact layout, page references, or visual fidelity. If a PDF is the only public source, a reflowed reader can be a good middle path for reading the words.

Keep the original file or link for anything that needs verification. A better reading surface should not erase the provenance of what you are reading.

Where Paper is today

Paper currently supports public, selectable-text PDF links. EPUB and standard book links are part of the longer-term direction, not supported formats today. The boundary is explained on the formats page.
